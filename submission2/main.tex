% DRAFT (2026-10-15), ~220 words. Every number is from logs.txt sections 14-17 /
% Tables I-IV. The last sentence stands in for the performance results: replace it

% e.g. "Vectorizing ... and compiling ... reduce runtime by X-Y x; running
% independent runs in parallel on the hybrid cores scales to Z x on N cores."
Quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms (QIEAs) run on classical hardware, but
they have rarely been tested on real routing problems with more than three
objectives, and little is known about where their runtime goes. We study
HQIEA-ARGC, which combines a qubit-angle chromosome with MOEA/D-style
decomposition and an adaptive rotation-gate control (ARGC) meant to escape
stagnation, on a 5-objective capacitated vehicle routing model of municipal waste
collection: distance, time, cost, load-dependent emissions, and workload balance.
Decoding the qubits by sorting their angles and splitting the tour by capacity
turns every chromosome into a feasible solution without repair. We evaluate on
three real road networks of Sibiu, Romania (198--333 collection points) and four
CVRPLIB instances, against NSGA-II, SPEA2, MOEA/D and RVEA over 30 runs of 500
generations. A generation-wise trace shows that the ARGC trigger almost never
fires, so hypervolume stops improving early. Adding a restart gated on archive
growth raises hypervolume by 28--66\%, significantly on all seven instances. Even
so, HQIEA-ARGC ranks last on every instance, at 30--58\% of the best
hypervolume, with MOEA/D and RVEA the hardest to match. A runtime profile on a
hybrid-core laptop CPU shows that fitness evaluation takes only 29--73\% of the
runtime, which caps the speedup from parallel evaluation alone at
1.4--3.7$\times$; most of the remaining time is function-call overhead. Removing
this serial overhead should therefore come before parallelizing the evaluation.
\end{abstract}
